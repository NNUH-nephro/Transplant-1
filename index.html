Pre-Incision Coordination & Combined Time-Out (Both Rooms)

Focus: Synchronised workflow across two surgical rooms before incision to minimise ischaemia times.
1.1 Sign In (Donor Room) — Before Induction of Anaesthesia
Donor Identity & Consent Verification Confirm donor identity, signed consent for nephrectomy, and laterality (left vs right) verbally with the donor and against chart documents.
Immunological Parameter Check ABO compatibility and final cross-match result documented and physically available in both rooms.
Donor Angiography Display Donor imaging (CT angiography / 3D reconstruction) displayed in the OR: number of renal arteries, veins, ureteral anatomy, and variants flagged.
Laterality Confirmation Which kidney is being retrieved is confirmed against imaging and marked (left preferred when anatomy is equivalent, due to longer renal vein).
Anaesthesia & Prophylaxis Review Anaesthesia machine, airway equipment, and IV access checked; antibiotic prophylaxis timing confirmed.
Thromboprophylaxis Deployment Sequential compression devices / DVT prophylaxis applied and verified functioning.
Blood Product Reserve Blood products cross-matched and available per institutional protocol.
Inter-Room Communication Line Direct communication line established between donor room and recipient room (phone/intercom) confirmed working.
1.2 Combined Time Out — Before Donor Skin Incision
Independent Verbal Confirmation Both operating rooms independently confirm patient identity, procedure, and site/laterality aloud.
Recipient Room Synchronization Recipient room confirms recipient is ready to be brought back / anaesthesia induction timing coordinated so that cold ischaemia time is minimised.
Dissection Alignment Communication Estimated donor nephrectomy-to-extraction time communicated to recipient team so iliac dissection can be timed to finish close to graft arrival.
Anatomical Variant Review Anticipated critical steps, blood loss risk, and any donor-specific anatomical variants reviewed by the donor team.
Recipient Status Briefing Recipient team confirms dialysis access status, last dialysis session, and volume status.
Immunosuppression Induction Sync Immunosuppression induction agent given/timed per protocol in recipient room.
Back-Table Verification Back-table set up, ice slush, preservation solution (HTK, UW, etc.), and instrument tray confirmed present and sterile.

3.1 - 3.4 Stepwise Laparoscopic Dissection Workflow
Access & Diagnostic Laparoscopy Establish pneumoperitoneum (12–15 mmHg). Place periumbilical camera port and 2-3 working ports under vision lateral to rectus. Run diagnostic scan.
Colon Mobilisation (White Line of Toldt) Incise line of Toldt; reflect colon medially to expose Gerota's fascia. Perform splenic flexure mobilisation only as needed to save time. Trace gonadal vein.
Ureter & Lower Pole Isolation Preserve ureter en bloc with peri-ureteral tissue and gonadal vein to safeguard blood supply. Mobilise lower pole, identifying any lower-pole accessory arteries.
Upper Pole & Hilar Skeletonisation Left side: divide adrenal vein early. Skeletonise renal artery and vein free of lymphatic/areolar tissue. Reconfirm findings against CT angiography.
3.6 - 3.8 Vascular Division, Organ Extraction & Closure
Pre-Clamping Diuresis and Extraction Setup Confirm kidney is attached only by hilum and ureter. Divide ureter distally with adequate periureteral envelope. Administer IV hydration bolus + mannitol/furosemide.
Vascular Transection Milestone Divide renal artery first (stapler or multiple Hem-o-loks). Divide renal vein as distally as possible to retain maximal length. Announce time of artery division aloud (Start of WIT).
Entrapment & Cold Immersion Extract kidney through Pfannenstiel/periumbilical incision via bag without delay. Immerge instantly into iced slush station. Log Cold Immersion Time (End of WIT).
Fossa Inspection & Closure Inspect renal fossa for hidden venous bleeding at reduced pneumoperitoneum pressure. Close incisions in layers; confirm correct counts.
Section 3

TRANSPLANT SURGERY & BACK-TABLE CHECKLIST

Focus: Allograft flushing, structural preparation, recipient exposure, and vascular anastomosis execution.
4.1 - 4.3 Back-Table Setup, Organ Prep & Handoff
Gravity Perfusion Milestone Flush via renal artery under gentle gravity pressure with pre-cooled preservation solution (UW / HTK) until venous effluent runs completely clear.
Graft Tailoring & Multi-Vessel Strategy Trim fat conservatively protecting hilar pad and ureteral branches. Assess accessory vessels; execute venous lengthening if managing a right-graft/short vein.
Dual-Room Handoff Verification Verify signed consent forms in charts. Confirm vessel count, structural anomalies, and transport metrics directly between surgical teams.
5.1 - 5.3 Recipient Room Setup & Exposure
Positioning & Bladder Distension Supine positioning; place Foley catheter. Distend bladder with sterile saline and clamp catheter to facilitate visualization during implantation.
Extraperitoneal Fossa Isolation Approach fossa via curvilinear lower-quadrant incision. Mobilise external/common iliac artery and vein. Ligate all peri-iliac lymphatics to avoid lymphoceles.
6.2 - 6.3 Vascular Implantation & Reperfusion
Veno-Venous Implantation Execute running end-to-side veno-venous anastomosis (donor renal vein to recipient external iliac vein) using double-armed monofilament fine suture.
Artero-Arterial Implantation Perform end-to-side arterial anastomosis to external or common iliac artery (favoured over internal iliac by EAU guidelines due to plaque variations). Log CIT and Rewarming/Anast WIT.
Graft Reperfusion Assessment Optimize hemodynamics with anaesthesia. Release venous clamp first, then arterial. Verify turgor, pink color change, palpable pulse, Doppler signals, and absence of leaks.
Section 4

UROLOGY RECONSTRUCTION CHECKLIST

Focus: Allograft ureteral preparation, bladder reconstruction, anti-reflux stent deployment, and drainage control.
Phase A: Pre-Anastomosis Preparation
Ureteral Viability Assessment Inspect the extracted donor ureter. Verify excellent micro-vascular blood supply via active adventitial bleeding and ensure there is no twisting or structural kinking.
Bladder Exposure & Distension Distend the recipient bladder using sterile saline via a Foley catheter to expose the anterior-lateral bladder wall safely.
Stent Configuration Preparation Confirm the presence of the specified Double-J (DJ) ureteral stent (typically 4.7 French, 14-16 cm) on the sterile field.
Phase B: Urinary Reconstruction Milestones
Ureteroneocystostomy Execution Create the ureter-to-bladder anastomosis using the designated surgical technique (EAU favoured extravesical Lich-Gregoir approach).
Anti-Reflux Mechanism Tunneling Incise detrusor muscle without breaching mucosa to construct an adequate submucosal tunnel over the ureter to prevent retrograde urine flow post-operatively.
DJ Stent Deployment Complete mucosa-to-mucosa spatulated suturing over the indwelling DJ stent, ensuring appropriate proximal loop placement in renal pelvis and distal loop in bladder.
Watertight Anastomosis Check Inspect the bladder detrusor closure line line for absolute watertight integrity. Test bladder with fluid to ensure zero leaks.
Deep Surgical Drain Placement Place a deep Jackson-Pratt (JP) closed suction drain adjacent to the vascular and ureteral anastomosis sites to monitor for early hematoma or urine leak.
Section 5

INTENSIVE CARE UNIT (ICU) RECIPIENT RECOVERY

Focus: High-acuity reverse isolation, hourly fluid balance synchronization, and hemodynamic perfusion management.
Phase A: Immediate Bedside Admission & Isolation Barrier
Dedicated Single Room Protocol Confirm the recipient is admitted directly into the pre-prepared Single Isolation Room. Verify active isolation signage on the door.
Reverse Isolation Engineering Controls Verify PPE cart at threshold, restricted visitor logging, and assigned one-to-one nursing staff allocation.
Monitoring Line Attachment Connect all invasive monitor lines immediately upon transfer: arterial line for continuous blood pressure tracking, CVC for central venous pressure tracking, and Foley line.
Phase B: Critical Post-Operative Hourly Protocols
Hourly Fluid Resuscitation Dynamics Measure and log Urine Output (UOP) every 60 minutes. Administer IV replacement infusions (Normal Saline or 0.45% Saline) hour-by-hour calculated exactly as: Previous Hour UOP + Ordered Baseline Fluid Volume (mL-for-mL replacement).
Hemodynamic Optimization for Perfusion Maintain continuous Mean Arterial Pressure (MAP) target at ≥ 80–85 mmHg to avoid allograft hypoperfusion. Titrate ordered IV crystalloids or low-dose vasopressors accordingly.
Surgical Drain Monitoring Track Jackson-Pratt (JP) drain outputs hourly. Document color and volume; immediately report changes indicating active bleeding or clear high-volume output (suspected urine leak).
Emergency Bedside Doppler Ultrasound Confirm baseline Bedside Doppler Ultrasound of the renal allograft is performed by Radiology within the first 2–4 hours post-arrival to verify patent arterial and venous blood flow.

Section 8: Vascular Anastomosis Decision Support
ScenarioTypical ChallengeTechnical Adaptation
Left donor kidneyLong renal vein, generally straightforwardStandard end-to-side veno-venous anastomosis to external iliac vein.
Right donor kidney — short veinIncreases tension/kinking risk; higher historical venous complication ratesDivide vein as distally as possible on donor side; consider gonadal vein patch, cryopreserved allograft, or common iliac venotomy.
Multiple renal arteriesRisk of segmental ischaemia if a branch is missedEx vivo reconstruction: common ostium/Carrel patch where anatomy allows, or separate sequential anastomoses safeguarding lower pole ureteral branches.
Very short or injured vessels / redo casesInadequate length for tension-free anastomosisInterposition graft (cryopreserved vascular allograft or autologous vein) or proximal anastomosis relocations.
Section 9: Troubleshooting Tips & Pitfalls
  • Donor Side: Reduce pneumoperitoneum pressure before final inspection of the renal fossa—venous oozing is often hidden at 12–15 mmHg. Preserve gonadal vein and periureteral tissue en bloc to safeguard blood supply.
  • Back Table: Keep the graft completely immersed in iced slush at all times except during active tailoring steps. Trim hilar fat conservatively to prevent lymphocele risks and protect small vessels.
  • Recipient Side: Confirm final graft orientation by provisionally sitting the kidney in the iliac fossa before running the final anastomotic stitches. Notify anaesthesiology before de-clamping so that volume and pressure are optimised at reperfusion to mitigate DGF. Ligate rather than simply cauterise peri-iliac lymphatics to mitigate lymphocele risks.
